# Current claims and evidence

This map distinguishes the mathematical claims, the recorded experimental effects and the limits of numerical verification. [The manuscript](../../paper/paper.tex), [supplement](../../paper/supplementary_derivations.tex) and [generated display lineage](../../paper/figures_revision/r1_lineage.json) provide the final locations and authenticated sources.

| Claim | Evidence and location | Scope |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Changing the response changes normalized AUC for the same predictions. | Main response identity; Figure 1B/C; Supplementary Equations S1-S2. | Exact self-reference, positive all-baseline squared residual, identical intervention weights. No IG-accuracy premise is needed. |
| N's area is no higher than Q's on an in-range curve. | Identity and positive-weight argument. | Strict inequality needs a positive-weight residual strictly between zero and the baseline residual. Endpoints alone give equality. |
| The saved schedule bounds the paired difference above by 0.2175. | Supplementary normalized-response proof. | All defined curves; positive finite stabilization tightens the fixed-baseline bound. It is not a claim of an attainable policy maximum. |
| Extreme negative differences require large residual ratios. | Supplementary tail-bound section. | The thresholds follow mathematically from the identity. They are not independent evidence for an empirical cause. |
| Small baseline distances and first-call concentration are consistent with a tail mechanism. | Results and Supplementary Figure S1. | Observational associations; whether partially replaced inputs cause distribution shift remains untested. |
| All eight saved-case medians are positive while six means are negative. | Figure 2A/B; Supplementary Table S1. | Two unmatched saved runs, two input types and two directions, 750 calls per case. Three negative-mean intervals span zero. |
| The two saved runs share tail-driving episodes. | Results: the same seven of thirty episodes produce vision-insertion differences below minus 100. | Related tails are not independent confirmations. Shared simulator seeds do not make evaluated contexts matched. |
| The illustrative 0.40 decision changes for identical predictions. | Introduction: Q-ranked vision-deletion medians 0.448 and 0.291. | An example of an absolute decision rule, not a validated cutoff. |
| Target choice can reverse exact IG ranking. | Figure 1D and Supplementary Equations S3-S6. | Constructed two-feature function, not an observed PickCube reversal; finite-epsilon ranking is proved. N bar heights use numerical integration. |
| Response choice alone can reverse which fixed ordering scores better. | Supplementary three-feature construction. | Mathematical possibility with common endpoints and fixed predictions, distinct from empirical winner estimates. |
| Random-order AUC need not equal 0.5. | Additive equal-feature construction. | The saved schedule gives 0.65976 under Q and 0.5 under unstabilized N for both directions. These are analytic values, not measured robot controls. |
| N-target IG exceeds Q-target IG under both responses in the primary study. | Table 2A: seven preplanned contrasts on sixty pretrained-PickCube episodes. | Both exceed random under N; neither separates from random under Q. The joint target-and-response contrast is inconclusive. |
| Exploratory preferred methods depend on the aggregation criterion. | Table 2B and Supplementary Table S3, all twenty method cells. | Raw-mean vision-deletion winners differ in all three groups; scaled means and episode medians agree in both PickCube groups and differ in StackCube. Only one of twelve vision top-two comparisons is clear; three of twenty cells overall are clear. |
| Simple fixed orders can exceed attribution-method means. | Supplementary Table S4, including raw/scaled means, episode medians and positive shares. | Three vision cells; tail sensitivity and changes under scaling prevent calling a raw-mean winner generally superior. |
| Bootstrap winner frequencies measure sample stability. | Supplementary Table S7; retrospective report. | 10,000 whole-episode draws, seed 930011, matched Q/N samples, explicit ties. They are not probabilities of a true population winner. |
| Successful episodes also overshoot. | Figure 2C/D and Supplementary Table S6. | Saved task-trained PickCube: 15 successful and 45 failed episodes, 74 and 1,125 calls. Matched task-trained group: 31 and 9 episodes, 62 and 18 calls. Different populations and unequal exposure prevent causal interpretation. |
| The failed matched group's negative deletion median is uncertain. | Supplementary Table S6 and main Results. | Nine episodes; the 95% episode-bootstrap interval includes zero. No sign interpretation is made. |
| Original saved identity information has distinct evidence statuses. | Main Methods and Supplementary Table S2. | Recorded, recovered, inferred and unknown are not interchangeable; missing observations/noise prevent regenerating the original runs. |
| Precision comparisons use matched populations. | Figure 3 and Supplementary Table S5. | Twelve contexts for matched completeness, eight of those with stored 16-bit rule-comparison maps, 120 for original-estimator comparisons on new contexts. The qualification sample is another eight-context population. |
| Finite-budget numerical agreement is not proof of accuracy. | Supplementary Table S5: Q 15/24 and N 5/24 qualification passes. | Close adaptive/Gauss-Legendre agreement does not override failed three-way qualification. All 815,210 evaluated nodes are finite; saved nonfinite counts are unknown. |
| Approximately first-order trapezoidal behavior occurs over tested budgets. | Main linear example and supplement endpoint-transition analysis. | Fixed positive epsilon gives a smooth integrand; sufficiently fine grids recover second-order behavior. |
| Original offline diagnostics show possible numerical failures. | Supplementary Figure S4 and Table S5. | Twelve completed contexts, six episodes, four pretrained-model tasks; distinct from 21 preserved-context reruns. No population failure rate or original-ranking validation follows. |
| Completeness and grid agreement can hide coordinate errors. | Smooth aliasing construction in the supplement. | Constructed cancellation and unresolved variation; exact integration also need not optimize perturbation scores. |

Original datasets, scientific code, protocols and result records remain preserved. New reporting is identified separately. Final document and package checks bind exact output bytes; a previous artifact's passing result does not certify a later rewrite.

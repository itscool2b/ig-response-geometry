Attribution evaluation depends on both the ranking that selects interventions and the response that scores them. We analyze this distinction for Integrated Gradients (IG) of a fixed-noise diffusion policy's predicted action chunk. For quadratic and stabilized negative-L2 action-discrepancy targets, we derive an exact normalized-response relation and the positive, path-dependent factor relating their gradients. A smooth construction reverses the IG ranking despite pointwise collinearity of the nonzero gradients. A finite-grid counterexample also rules out a universal random-ranking AUC reference of 0.5. Reanalysis of 99 preserved Robotics Diffusion Transformer files shows the practical effect: changing only the response changes median vision deletion AUC from 0.4479 to 0.2908 for the same recorded quadratic-target rankings. Across eight paired rescoring cases, every median response change is positive, but overshooting tails make six means negative. Exploratory episode-omission checks leave insertion-mean signs unchanged but can reverse language-deletion signs. A second smooth construction shows that several integration grids can agree in completeness, ranking and perturbation curves while reversing the exact IG order; the inaccurate order even scores better on both perturbation metrics in this example. Existing actual-model diagnostics likewise separate repeatability from unresolved integration-budget sensitivity in an incomplete cohort. These results establish distinct roles for response geometry, numerical accuracy and ranking quality. They do not qualify a production numerical setting, demonstrate superior attribution rankings, or explain task success or learned-weight dependence. The artifact preserves the raw-record lineage, analytic checks, adverse results and provenance limits.

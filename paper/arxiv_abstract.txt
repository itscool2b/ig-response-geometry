Attribution rankings select inputs for interventions, and their evaluations also need responses that score those interventions. We study this distinction for Integrated Gradients (IG) attributions of an action chunk predicted by a diffusion policy with fixed noise. For quadratic and stabilized negative-L2 action-discrepancy targets, we give an exact relation between their normalized responses. We also derive a positive, path-dependent factor between their gradients. Using a smooth construction, we show that IG rankings can reverse while the nonzero gradients remain pointwise collinear. A finite-grid counterexample shows that 0.5 cannot serve as a universal random-ranking AUC reference. Reanalyzing 99 preserved Robotics Diffusion Transformer files, we find that changing only the evaluation response changes median vision deletion AUC from 0.4479 to 0.2908 although the recorded quadratic-target rankings stay the same. In all eight paired rescoring cases, every median response change is positive although six means are negative because of overshooting tails. Exploratory episode-omission results retain insertion-mean signs but can reverse language-deletion signs. Another smooth construction gives several integration grids with the same completeness, ranking and perturbation curves but an ordering that reverses the exact IG order. In this example, the inaccurate ordering scores better on both perturbation metrics. Diagnostics on the actual model separate repeatability from unresolved integration-budget sensitivity in an incomplete cohort. Together, the results separate response-geometry effects from attribution-ranking quality and numerical-integration accuracy. They do not qualify a production numerical setting, demonstrate better attribution rankings, or explain task success or dependence on learned weights. The artifact retains raw-record lineage, analytic checks, adverse results and provenance limits.

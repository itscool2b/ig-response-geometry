Attribution evaluation depends on both the ranking that selects interventions and the response that scores them. We analyze this distinction for Integrated Gradients (IG) of a fixed-noise diffusion policy's predicted action chunk. For quadratic and stabilized negative-L2 action-discrepancy targets, we derive an exact normalized-response relation and the positive, path-dependent factor relating their gradients. A smooth two-feature construction reverses the IG ranking despite pointwise collinearity of the nonzero gradients, including at the implemented stabilizer. On the historical nine-point grid, a 100-feature equal-contribution policy gives quadratic-response insertion and deletion AUC of 0.65976 for every ordering, ruling out a universal random-ranking reference of 0.5. A canonical reanalysis of 99 saved Robotics Diffusion Transformer files then separates fixed-ranking response changes from unauthenticated cross-target comparisons. For the same recorded quadratic-target rankings, changing only the response changes median deletion AUC from 0.4479 to 0.2908 without changing a selected feature. Conversely, quadratic rescoring changes the recorded L2 rankings' deletion AUC from 0.2830 to 0.4404. Existing actual-model diagnostics additionally show repeatable computations with unresolved integration-budget sensitivity in an explicitly incomplete cohort. They do not qualify a production numerical setting. Together these results distinguish response geometry, numerical reliability and ranking quality. They establish neither improved attribution rankings nor explanations of task success or learned-weight dependence. The artifact links reconstructed results to preserved records, retains provenance limitations and numerical failures, and provides the analytic checks.

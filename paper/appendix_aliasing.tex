\section{A Smooth Counterexample to Nested-Grid Agreement}
\label{sec:appendix-aliasing}

Agreement between finitely many integration grids, even with exact completeness
and unchanged perturbation curves, does not by itself certify the coordinate
integrals. The following construction isolates this limitation without a model
experiment. All equalities in the construction are statements in real arithmetic.

For a positive integer $M$, define the smooth scalar policy and target
\begin{align}
 h(t)&=\frac25\sin^2(\pi M t),\\
 \mu(x,y)&=\frac25x+\frac35y+(x-y)h\!\left(\frac{x+y}{2}\right),\\
 Q(x,y)&=-\frac12\bigl(\mu(x,y)-1\bigr)^2.
\end{align}
Take baseline $(0,0)$, actual input $(1,1)$ and the fixed own-reference action
$\mu(1,1)=1$. This has the same quadratic self-reference form as the main target,
with one active action entry and $\sigma^2=1$.
Along the straight path $(t,t)$, the transverse term in $\mu$ vanishes, so
$\mu(t,t)=t$, $Q(t,t)=-(1-t)^2/2$, and
\begin{equation}
 \nabla Q(t,t)=(1-t)\left(\frac25+h(t),\frac35-h(t)\right).
 \label{eq:aliasing-gradient}
\end{equation}
The endpoint score gap is $1/2$. Both coordinate integrals are positive and
their exact values are distinct.

Let $T_m$ denote componentwise composite trapezoidal integration with $m$
intervals. Whenever $m$ divides $M$, every node $t=k/m$ has $h(t)=0$.
The remaining coordinate functions are linear, hence
\begin{equation}
 T_m=\left(\frac15,\frac3{10}\right),\qquad
 \mathbf{1}^{\mathsf T}T_m=\frac12=Q(1,1)-Q(0,0).
 \label{eq:aliasing-coarse}
\end{equation}
Thus all such grids have zero completeness residual and the same strict
absolute-coordinate ranking, with feature 2 first. They also generate identical
insertion and deletion masks under a fixed deterministic ranking rule. Evaluating
those masks on the same policy gives identical response curves for every such
grid, regardless of whether its coordinate integrals are accurate.

In contrast, $h(1-t)=h(t)$ and $\int_0^1h(t)\,dt=1/5$, giving
\begin{equation}
 \int_0^1(1-t)h(t)\,dt=\frac1{10},\qquad
 \operatorname{IG}(Q)=\left(\frac3{10},\frac15\right).
 \label{eq:aliasing-exact}
\end{equation}
The exact ranking puts feature 1 first. The coarse-grid errors are
$(-1/10,1/10)$: their sum cancels exactly, while their coordinate $L_1$ norm is
$1/5$, or $40\%$ of the exact map's $L_1$ norm. The path score itself is a
quadratic polynomial; unresolved variation lies in its coordinate-gradient
decomposition, which a scalar completeness check cannot recover.

For this particular construction, the $2M$ grid resolves the discrepancy:
its sampled $h$ values alternate between $0$ and $2/5$, so $T_{2M}h=1/5$.
Grid symmetry gives $T_{2M}[(1-t)h]=\tfrac12T_{2M}h=1/10$, recovering
Equation~\ref{eq:aliasing-exact}. This is not a universal sufficient doubling
rule. For any fixed finite list of uniform interval counts, choosing $M$ as a
common multiple places all of those grids in Equation~\ref{eq:aliasing-coarse}.
The example supplies no derivative bound that would otherwise control the
unresolved variation.

\paragraph{Exact integration and perturbation quality are different questions.}
For the displayed $M=128$, $h(1/2)=0$, so the whole-feature interventions have
$\mu(1,0)=2/5$ and $\mu(0,1)=3/5$.
With the same normalized $Q$ response and grid $\{0,1/2,1\}$, the aliased
feature-2-first ordering has insertion ordinates $(0,0.84,1)$ and deletion
ordinates $(1,0.64,0)$, giving AUCs $0.67$ and $0.57$.
The exact-IG feature-1-first ordering instead has insertion AUC $0.57$ and
deletion AUC $0.67$.
Thus the inaccurate map's ranking scores better under both conventional
preferences, larger insertion AUC and smaller deletion AUC, in this toy.
The finite off-path interventions measure a different property from the
diagonal path integral: the transverse policy term changes its coordinate
gradients without changing these Boolean intervention values.
Neither completeness nor exact integration guarantees an optimal perturbation
score. This is a toy-specific separation, not evidence of RDT ranking efficacy.

The standalone artifact
\texttt{analysis/revision/nested\_grid\_aliasing.py} evaluates the unchanged
production trapezoidal IG kernel on this CPU toy with $M=128$, in FP32 and
FP64. Budgets $32,64,128$ reproduce the incorrect order, whereas $256$ reproduces
the analytic order to numerical tolerance. The report retains measured
coordinate errors, completeness residuals, repeated-run equality and common
response curves; it does not replace finite-precision results with the exact
equalities above. The two-coordinate example uses no RDT weights or observations.
It explains why finite-grid agreement alone is insufficient for certification,
not why any particular PickSingleYCB diagnostic failed, and approves no
production integration setting.

This supplement gives the proofs, experimental details and complete numerical tables supporting the main paper. Q scores negative squared action distance and N scores negative stabilized distance. The squared distance from the fixed reference action is $r$; replacing all features gives $B$. Constructed examples establish mathematical possibilities, while the experimental tables describe their stated recorded populations. These two forms of evidence are kept separate.

\section{Response identities and target-induced reversal}
\label{sec:proofs}

\subsection{Normalized response difference}
\label{sec:response-proof}

The identity proves how the normalized response changes while intervention predictions remain fixed. Exact self-reference means that the actual input reproduces the reference prediction, giving $r(x)=0$. Assume $B>0$. Let $\eta=\sqrt{\epsilon}$ be the stabilizer's square root, $b=\sqrt{B+\epsilon}$ the stabilized all-baseline distance and $z=\sqrt{r+\epsilon}$ the stabilized intermediate distance. Then $B=(b-\eta)(b+\eta)$ and
\begin{equation}
C_Q-C_N=\frac{b^2-z^2}{B}-\frac{b-z}{b-\eta}
=\frac{(z-\eta)(b-z)}{B}.
\end{equation}
For $\eta\le z\le b$, the product is nonnegative and maximized at $z=(\eta+b)/2$, giving
\begin{equation}
0\le C_Q-C_N\le\frac{(b-\eta)^2}{4B}
=\frac{b-\eta}{4(b+\eta)}\le\frac14.
\end{equation}
For $z>b$, the difference is negative, so the $1/4$ upper bound holds for all $r\ge0$. Nonnegative AUC weights sum to one, yielding $\Delta\le1/4$ for every defined curve. On the nine-point schedule they are $(0.005,0.025,0.045,0.075,0.10,0.15,0.225,0.25,0.125)$. The endpoints contribute zero difference and have total weight 0.13, giving $\Delta\le0.87/4=0.2175$. For positive $\epsilon$ and finite $B$, the fixed-$B$ schedule bound $0.87(b-\eta)/[4(b+\eta)]$ is strictly smaller. Fully in-range curves additionally satisfy $\Delta\ge0$, strictly if a point with positive AUC weight has $0<r<B$. If $B=0$, normalization is undefined. When the 1\% and 5\% language values coincide, dropping the 1\% node changes their combined AUC weight by 0.02; the $1/4$ pointwise bound therefore bounds the paired change by $0.02/4=0.005$ for fully in-range calls when only that duplicate node is removed. This also describes the realized-fraction change for the twenty-token schedule. For sixteen or twenty-five tokens, other realized fractions move as well, so 0.005 does not bound their total nominal-to-realized change.

\subsection{Finite-stabilizer rank reversal}
\label{sec:reversal-proof}

This example shows that changing only the attributed target can reverse the exact IG ranking. It is a constructed possibility, not an observed PickCube reversal. Let $g(x_1,x_2)=x_1^2+\beta x_2$, with $\beta=4/5$, $x'=(0,0)$ and $x=(1,1)$. Along the diagonal, the scalar action residual and its square are
\[
\rho(\alpha)=(1-\alpha)(1+\alpha+\beta),\qquad r(\alpha,\alpha)=\rho(\alpha)^2.
\]
Since $\nabla g=(2\alpha,\beta)$ there, differentiating the targets for $d_a=1$ gives
\begin{equation}
\nabla Q=\rho(\alpha)(2\alpha,\beta),\qquad
\nabla N=\frac{\rho(\alpha)}{\sqrt{\rho(\alpha)^2+\epsilon}}(2\alpha,\beta).
\end{equation}
Polynomial integration yields
\begin{equation}
A^Q=\left(\frac12+\frac\beta3,\frac{2\beta}3+\frac{\beta^2}2\right)
=\left(\frac{23}{30},\frac{64}{75}\right),
\end{equation}
so feature 2 leads by $13/150$. Norm attributions tend to $(1,\beta)$ as $\epsilon$ decreases to zero.

For the positive stabilizer, define $\psi(\rho)=1-\rho/\sqrt{\rho^2+\epsilon}\ge0$. Its derivative is $-\epsilon/(\rho^2+\epsilon)^{3/2}<0$, and $\rho(\alpha)\ge\beta(1-\alpha)$. Therefore
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\int_0^1\psi(\rho(\alpha))\,d\alpha
&\le\int_0^1\left[1-\frac{\beta(1-\alpha)}{\sqrt{\beta^2(1-\alpha)^2+\epsilon}}\right]d\alpha\\
&=1-\frac{\sqrt{\beta^2+\epsilon}-\sqrt{\epsilon}}{\beta}
\le\frac{\sqrt{\epsilon}}{\beta}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The identities $1-A_1^N=\int_0^1 2\alpha\psi(\rho(\alpha))\,d\alpha$ and $\beta-A_2^N=\beta\int_0^1\psi(\rho(\alpha))\,d\alpha$, together with $0\le2\alpha\le2$ and $\epsilon=10^{-12}$, give
\begin{equation}
0\le1-A_1^N\le2.5\times10^{-6},\qquad
0\le\beta-A_2^N\le10^{-6}.
\end{equation}
Thus $A_1^N-A_2^N\ge0.1999975$. Every coordinate is positive, so ordinary and absolute-coordinate rankings agree. Only the target changes. At the half-feature insertion point, feature 1 first gives normalized Q response about 0.80 versus 0.69 for feature 2 first, and N response about 0.56 versus 0.44. Deletion swaps these intermediate values. Hence feature 1 first scores better under both responses, despite exact Q-target IG ranking feature 2 first.

\subsection{Finite-grid random-order calibration}
\label{sec:random-calibration}

This calculation shows why a random-order reference depends on the response, even when every input feature contributes equally to the action.

For $g(x)=\sum_{i=1}^n x_i$, an all-one input and zero baseline, every feature order gives residual distances $k$ after $k$ deletions and $n-k$ after $k$ insertions. At $q=k/n$, normalized Q curves are $1-q^2$ and $1-(1-q)^2$. Both have second derivative $-2$, so their trapezoidal areas underestimate their common integral $2/3$ by $1/(6n^2)$ on the full-prefix grid. Their identical curvature also gives identical quadrature errors on any common grid, including an asymmetric one. The nine-point schedule with $n=100$ gives area 0.65976. Unstabilized N gives the lines $1-q$ and $q$, both with trapezoidal area 0.5 on either schedule.

\subsection{A common response change can reorder rankings}
\label{sec:ranking-comparison}

The response can also reverse which of two fixed feature orders has the better score. The following construction holds both orders and their intervention predictions fixed.

Take three features and insertion fractions $\{0,1/3,2/3,1\}$, with $B=1$. Let orders $(1,2,3)$ and $(3,2,1)$ give residual sequences $(1,1/4,1/4,0)$ and $(1,16/25,0,0)$, respectively. Their intermediate subsets are distinct, so these values can belong to one scalar policy with the same endpoints. Q insertion AUCs are $2/3$ and $31/50=0.620$; with $\epsilon=0$, N AUCs are $1/2$ and $17/30\approx0.567$. Thus the better order reverses. The inequalities persist at $\epsilon=10^{-12}$. This establishes possibility, not a measured comparison of the saved cohorts.

\section{A smooth counterexample to integration-grid agreement}
\label{sec:aliasing}

\subsection{Unresolved coordinate variation}
\label{sec:aliasing-proof}

A smooth function can hide coordinate variation from every tested grid while its attribution sum remains exact. This example separates completeness and grid agreement from correct feature rankings.

For an integer $K>0$, define
\begin{align}
h(\alpha)&=\frac25\sin^2(\pi K\alpha),\\
g(x_1,x_2)&=\frac25x_1+\frac35x_2+
(x_1-x_2)h\!\left(\frac{x_1+x_2}{2}\right),\\
Q(x_1,x_2)&=-\frac12\bigl(g(x_1,x_2)-1\bigr)^2.
\end{align}
Take baseline $(0,0)$, actual input $(1,1)$, and fixed reference $g(1,1)=1$.
The transverse term vanishes along $(\alpha,\alpha)$, so
$g(\alpha,\alpha)=\alpha$ and $Q(\alpha,\alpha)=-(1-\alpha)^2/2$.
Differentiating before restricting to that path gives
\begin{equation}
\nabla Q(\alpha,\alpha)=(1-\alpha)
\left(\frac25+h(\alpha),\frac35-h(\alpha)\right).
\label{eq:aliasing-gradient}
\end{equation}
Thus the oscillation transfers gradient between coordinates while leaving their
sum unchanged.

Use the composite trapezoidal rule with $m$ intervals.
Whenever $m$ divides $K$, every node $\alpha=j/m$ has $h(\alpha)=0$.
The sampled coordinate functions are then linear. Both the trapezoidal rule and endpoint-inclusive average integrate a linear function exactly on any uniform grid, giving
\begin{equation}
A_m^{\mathrm{trap}}=\left(\frac15,\frac3{10}\right),\qquad
\mathbf{1}^{\mathsf T}A_m^{\mathrm{trap}}=\frac12=Q(1,1)-Q(0,0).
\label{eq:aliasing-coarse}
\end{equation}
Here $\mathbf{1}=(1,1)^{\mathsf T}$ sums the coordinates. Every such grid has zero
completeness residual and ranks feature 2 first, giving identical masks and
perturbation curves under a deterministic ranking rule.

The exact coordinate integrals differ. Since $h(1-\alpha)=h(\alpha)$,
substitution gives
\[
\int_0^1\alpha h(\alpha)\,d\alpha
=\int_0^1(1-\alpha)h(\alpha)\,d\alpha.
\]
Adding these expressions and using $\int_0^1 h(\alpha)\,d\alpha=1/5$
shows that either integral is $1/10$. Consequently,
\begin{equation}
A_{\mathrm{exact}}^Q=\left(\frac3{10},\frac15\right).
\label{eq:aliasing-exact}
\end{equation}
The exact order puts feature 1 first. Coarse errors $(-1/10,1/10)$ cancel in
completeness, but their $L_1$ norm, $1/5$, is 40\% of the exact attribution's norm.

On the $2K$ grid, $h$ alternates between zero and $2/5$, with trapezoidal integral
$1/5$. Reflection symmetry gives area $1/10$ for $(1-\alpha)h$, recovering
Equation~\ref{eq:aliasing-exact}. This recovery is construction-specific. For
finitely many uniform budgets, a common multiple $K$ hides every oscillation.
Agreement cannot certify coordinate integrals without a regularity bound on unresolved variation.

The endpoint-inclusive average has the same coarse failure.
When $m$ divides $K$, the average of the sampled linear functions also yields
Equation~\ref{eq:aliasing-coarse}. On the $2K$ grid, only odd nodes contribute
to $(1-\alpha)h$. Their sum is
$\frac25\sum_{\ell=0}^{K-1}(1-(2\ell+1)/(2K))=K/5$.
Dividing by the $2K+1$ nodes gives
\begin{equation}
A_{2K}^{\mathrm{avg}}=
\left(\frac15+\frac{K}{5(2K+1)},\
\frac3{10}-\frac{K}{5(2K+1)}\right).
\label{eq:aliasing-average}
\end{equation}
The first coordinate exceeds the second for $K>1/2$, including every allowed
integer. Coordinates remain inexact; exact $2K$ recovery belongs to the trapezoidal rule.

\subsection{Exact IG and perturbation quality}
\label{sec:aliasing-quality}

Recovering the exact integral need not optimize a chosen insertion or deletion score. The same construction makes that distinction explicit.

Set $K=128$, so $h(1/2)=0$ and the whole-feature interventions give
$g(1,0)=2/5$ and $g(0,1)=3/5$. Under the same normalized Q response
and fractions $\{0,1/2,1\}$, feature-2-first insertion values are
$(0,0.84,1)$ and deletion values are $(1,0.64,0)$.
Their trapezoidal AUCs are 0.67 and 0.57.
The exact-IG feature-1-first order instead gives insertion AUC 0.57 and deletion AUC 0.67.
The inaccurate order therefore scores better by both conventional criteria.
The transverse term alters coordinate gradients along the diagonal without
altering these finite off-path intervention values. Exact path integration
and perturbation-score optimization are distinct questions.

CPU fp32/fp64 checks at $K=128$ verify trapezoidal coordinates, completeness,
repeatability and responses: budgets 32, 64 and 128 alias; 256 recovers the analytic
order to numerical tolerance. The endpoint-inclusive result is analytic only;
these checks use no RDT data.

\section{Endpoint transitions and tail bounds}

The stabilized norm target has a narrow endpoint transition that explains the tested integration behavior. In the linear action example, write the remaining action distance as $t=1-\alpha$. The gradient magnitude is $t/\sqrt{t^2+\epsilon}$, and the exact integral is $\sqrt{1+\epsilon}-\sqrt\epsilon$. With $\epsilon=10^{-12}$, grids of 64 through 512 intervals do not resolve the transition near $t=0$. Assigning weight to its zero endpoint then produces approximately inverse-budget error. This describes the tested regime, not the asymptotic order for fixed positive epsilon. A sufficiently fine grid resolves the smooth transition and restores second-order trapezoidal convergence. Midpoint and Gauss-Legendre rules avoid endpoint nodes but still require accuracy checks for the particular integrand.

Some tail thresholds follow from the response identity and cannot be treated as independent observations. Put $R=\max r/B$ over interior nodes and $s=\sqrt R$. Assume $R>1$, as required for a negative $\Delta$. For an overshooting point, stabilization reduces the magnitude of the negative difference relative to its zero-stabilizer limit; the minimum is bounded below by $s(1-s)$. Since the saved schedule's interior weights sum to 0.87, $\Delta\ge0.87s(1-s)$. Consequently, for $a>0$, $\Delta<-a$ requires
\[
R>\left[\frac{1+\sqrt{1+4a/0.87}}{2}\right]^2.
\]
For $a=1$ and $a=100$ the required ratios are approximately 2.83 and 126.2. The empirical findings are the actual residual ratios, small baseline distances and concentration in particular calls, not the fact that these necessary thresholds hold. The sixteen correlation groups comprise eight saved pretrained PickCube cases, two pooled task-trained vision cases and six seed-specific task-trained language cases. Their negative rank correlations mean that larger residual ratios accompany more negative $\Delta$.

\section{Experimental details and complete results}

The following tables retain each population separately. The two saved pretrained PickCube runs use different ranking targets and language baselines; shared simulator seeds do not make them matched experiments. The saved task-trained PickCube primary sample pools seeds 42, 142 and 242, with 20 episodes each, respectively 320, 480 and 399 calls, and 9, 1 and 5 successes. The separate 334-call rerun is a sensitivity sample and is excluded from the 1,199-call primary total. Each saved additional task pools two eight-episode runs at 128 intervals. The 800 language curves used to check distinct StackCube 1\%/5\% responses comprise two 200-call runs and both intervention directions.

The offline diagnostic collection contains twelve completed contexts per input type and target from six episodes. The preserved bank used in the matched numerical campaign contains the same 21 available identifiers from that collection's 24 planned identifiers. It has four pretrained contexts each for PickCube, StackCube, PegInsertionSide and PickSingleYCB, two task-trained PickCube contexts and three task-trained StackCube contexts, from twelve source episodes overall. Only twelve pretrained contexts had complete offline diagnostic results at the collection's sealed cutoff. These counts describe different subsets and must not be combined as independent episodes. Small complete-case strata have at most two episodes. Nominal 95\% percentile intervals contain the known value in only about 9\%--50\% of the examined small-sample simulations; those strata support no population-level conclusion.

Saved analyses use the nominal percentage axis and bootstrap seed zero. Matched analyses use the actual fractions selected by stored masks and seed 930011, with nominal-axis sensitivity results retained. The endpoint grids share 513 gradient samples, allowing nested trapezoidal and equal-weight estimates through 512 intervals and midpoint estimates through 256. Original-estimator sums can depend on GPU summation order; the separate 32-bit-sum alternative remains recorded. Repeated saved IG sums can differ despite identical target changes, which is another reason endpoint agreement does not authenticate the missing original computation. Unstabilized RMS action distance normalizes to the same curve as unstabilized N and supplies no third independent normalized metric.
